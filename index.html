Introduction

Freshwater ecosystems, especially rivers, are vital for biodiversity, human health, and local communities. However, pollution, urbanization, and improper waste disposal can contribute to declining water quality in many areas, including communities around Calapan City.

Traditional water-quality monitoring often relies on manual sampling and laboratory analysis. While these approaches can provide useful measurements, they may be time-consuming, labor-intensive, and unable to provide continuous updates.

AquaSense proposes a solar-powered IoT-based sensor network that monitors pH, turbidity, and temperature. The concept explores how more frequent readings could help identify changes earlier and support informed environmental monitoring.

Methodology

Research Design

This study utilizes a Quantitative Research Design with a descriptive component. It focuses on gathering numerical data through the AquaSense sensor network to determine the current water quality parameters (pH, Turbidity, Temperature) of the freshwater ecosystem.

The design is subject to the final approved methodology of the institution, ensuring that data gathering and analysis strictly follow academic and scientific standards.

Locale of the Study

The proposed deployment and field evaluation will be conducted at:

  • Barangay Ibaba West, Calapan City, Oriental Mindoro.

This specific freshwater site was chosen due to its ecological significance to the local community. Any actual field deployment, water sampling, and physical evaluation should be properly documented and coordinated with local authorities.

Participants and Evaluators

The intended subject-matter evaluators for this prototype may include:

  • Science and IT Teachers
  • Qualified Environmental Reviewers
  • Local Environmental Officers (if applicable)

Their role is to assess the accuracy, functionality, and feasibility of the AquaSense prototype according to the approved study protocol and rubrics of the institution.

Proposed Prototype (AquaSense)

The system is composed of the following key components:

  • Solar-powered floating platform: Allows autonomous deployment in water.
  • ESP32-based controller: The brain of the system for data processing.
  • Sensor Array: Industrial-grade pH, Turbidity, and Temperature sensors.
  • Cloud Transmission: Sends data via WiFi to Firebase Realtime Database.
  • Web Dashboard: Provides real-time visualization of water quality data.

Data Collection and Analysis

To ensure reliable and accurate readings, the following procedures are planned:

  • Sensor Validation: Comparing AquaSense readings against standard laboratory instruments.
  • Repeatability Checks: Placing sensors in the same water sample multiple times to check consistency.
  • Power Measurements: Monitoring battery and solar panel efficiency over time.
  • Network & Transmission Tests: Evaluating WiFi range and Firebase data logging reliability.

Data collected will be transmitted every 3 minutes and stored in the cloud for trend analysis and historical review.

Conclusion

AquaSense presents a proposed solar-powered IoT approach to monitoring selected freshwater parameters: pH, turbidity, and temperature. By combining sensors, a microcontroller, wireless communication, and a web dashboard, the concept aims to make readings easier to review and support timely follow-up.

The dashboard now displays live data streamed from the ESP32 sensor unit via Firebase Realtime Database. Readings update automatically and are stored for historical review.

Future work may include physical deployment, calibration against reference instruments, extended durability and power testing, connectivity evaluation, and field validation with appropriate permissions.
