Introduction

Freshwater ecosystems, especially rivers, are vital for biodiversity, human health, and local communities. However, pollution, urbanization, and improper waste disposal can contribute to declining water quality in many areas, including communities around Calapan City.

Traditional water-quality monitoring often relies on manual sampling and laboratory analysis. While these approaches can provide useful measurements, they may be time-consuming, labor-intensive, and unable to provide continuous updates.

AquaSense proposes a solar-powered IoT-based sensor network that monitors pH, turbidity, and temperature. The concept explores how more frequent readings could help identify changes earlier and support informed environmental monitoring.

Conclusion

AquaSense presents a proposed solar-powered IoT approach to monitoring selected freshwater parameters: pH, turbidity, and temperature. By combining sensors, a microcontroller, wireless communication, and a web dashboard, the concept aims to make readings easier to review and support timely follow-up.

The current webpage is a presentation prototype. Its changing values are simulated, so they do not demonstrate verified sensor accuracy, live connectivity, or actual water conditions.

Future work may include physical deployment, calibration against reference instruments, extended durability and power testing, connectivity evaluation, and field validation with appropriate permissions.
