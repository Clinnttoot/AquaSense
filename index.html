Project Overview BioSphere Innovations · STEM Strand · DWCC Calapan

AquaSense is a solar-powered, Internet of Things (IoT)-based sensor network designed to continuously monitor the freshwater quality of Barangay Ibaba West, Calapan City, Oriental Mindoro. It measures three key physicochemical parameters — pH level, turbidity, and temperature — and transmits real-time data to an online dashboard, addressing the delays and limitations of conventional laboratory-based water quality monitoring.

⚡

Real-Time Monitoring

Continuous, automated data collection every 3 seconds — eliminating the lag time of manual sampling and laboratory analysis.

☀️

Solar-Powered

10W solar panel with charge controller and 12V battery backup ensures off-grid operation and sustainable energy use.

📡

IoT-Connected

ESP32 microcontroller transmits sensor data to a cloud-based monitoring platform for remote access and early warning.

The Problem and Its Background Chapter I — Introduction

The Challenge

Degradation of freshwater sources is outpacing effective monitoring. The deterioration of water quality in many rivers and freshwater sources continues to diminish due to pollution, agricultural activities, waste dumping, and environmental changes — despite the significance of these resources in ecosystems and communities.

The degradation of these water resources leads to variation in crucial water quality parameters such as pH level, turbidity, and temperature, which can adversely affect aquatic organisms and communities dependent on these resources. The lack of water quality assessment creates issues for immediate identification and response to abrupt changes in water conditions.

A review article by Mishra et al. (2022) reveals that conventional monitoring of water quality primarily involves field sample collection and physical, chemical, and microbiological analysis in laboratories. The review further highlights that conventional water quality monitoring is labor-intensive and cost-intensive, and results may not be available for several days. Moreover, this kind of monitoring is not continuous, and the testing frequency can be low, making it difficult to provide real-time information.

The Solution

International studies refer to real-time IoT-based monitoring as one of the means for efficient water management. According to the UNESCO United Nations World Water Development Report (2024), freshwater ecosystems have continued to be at risk from increasing pollution, climate change, urbanization, and population growth — accentuating the need for essential monitoring and real-time information on water quality all over the world.

Sugiharto et al. (2024) stated that real-time monitoring systems can send water quality data to an online platform, ensuring accurate water quality data. Kumar et al. (2022) added that wireless sensor networks coupled with IoT technology can enhance accurate, reliable, and accessible water quality data via real-time monitoring and remote information transmission.

These studies demonstrate the value of continuous monitoring in detecting sudden changes in water conditions, aiding environmental decision-making, and supporting sustainable management and protection of freshwater resources.

Theoretical Framework

The AquaSense System is grounded on four theories that explain how it works and functions:

  • Sensor Theory (Fraden, 2016): Sensors detect physical or chemical changes in the environment and transduce them into electrical signals. The pH, turbidity, and temperature sensors are the core sensors of AquaSense that continually monitor physicochemical properties in water.
  • Internet of Things (IoT) Theory (Atzori et al., 2010): Physical devices embedded with sensors and communication technologies can collect, exchange, and transmit data over the internet without constant human-to-machine interaction. AquaSense uses IoT to send real-time water quality data to an online platform.
  • General System Theory (Bertalanffy, 1968): A system is a set of interrelated and dependent components operating toward a common goal. AquaSense integrates pH, turbidity, and temperature sensors, an ESP32 microcontroller, solar power with battery backup, and an IoT platform into one cohesive monitoring system.
  • Buoyancy Theory (Archimedes, c. 250 BCE): An object immersed in a fluid experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced (Fb = ρ·V·g). The buoyant force determines whether an object floats, which is relevant to the deployment of the sensor system on the water surface.

Conclusion Summary of Findings and Recommendations

"
AquaSense transforms freshwater monitoring from a delayed, labor-intensive process into a continuous, real-time system — empowering communities to detect water quality changes before they become environmental crises.

This study addressed the limitations of conventional water quality monitoring in Barangay Ibaba West, Calapan City, by designing and developing AquaSense — a solar-powered, IoT-based sensor network for real-time pH level, turbidity, and temperature assessment.

Conventional monitoring methods are labor-intensive, cost-intensive, and produce delayed results that cannot capture sudden environmental changes. AquaSense addresses these gaps by providing continuous, real-time water quality data through an ESP32 microcontroller integrated with pH, turbidity, and DS18B20 temperature sensors, powered by renewable solar energy with battery backup.

The system transmits water quality data to an online monitoring platform, enabling remote access and faster response to abrupt changes in water conditions. This supports the protection and conservation of the river ecosystem, raises community awareness, and provides local government units with a data-driven basis for environmental plans and conservation programs.

By combining renewable energy technology with IoT-based sensing, AquaSense demonstrates a practical, sustainable, and cost-effective approach to freshwater resource management at the local scale.

Expanded Monitoring Additional parameters such as dissolved oxygen, conductivity, and nutrient levels could be integrated into future iterations.
Scalability The system can be replicated across other priority river systems in Oriental Mindoro and beyond.
Community Integration Local government units and environmental stakeholders can adopt AquaSense for early warning systems and environmental planning.
Long-Term Sustainability Solar-powered operation with battery backup ensures continuous monitoring with minimal maintenance requirements.
